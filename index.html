What is a Kua number?

Your Kua number (also written Gua number, 命卦) is a single digit from 1 to 9 calculated from your birth year and gender in the Eight Mansions school of classical Chinese feng shui. It identifies your personal trigram from the Bagua, your Five Element, whether you belong to the East group (Kua 1, 3, 4, 9) or West group (Kua 2, 6, 7, 8), and the eight compass directions ranked by how their energy relates to yours.

Four directions are considered supportive — Sheng Qi, Tian Yi, Yan Nian and Fu Wei — and four are treated with caution: Jue Ming, Wu Gui, Liu Sha and Huo Hai.

How the calculation works

  1. Take the last two digits of your birth year and add them until you are left with a single digit. Example: 1987 → 8 + 7 = 15 → 1 + 5 = 6.
  2. Men born before 2000: subtract that digit from 10. Women born before 2000: add 5 and reduce to one digit.
  3. Born in 2000 or later: men subtract from 9; women add 6 and reduce to one digit. A result of 0 is read as 9 (this happens for men born in 2000, 2009, 2018 and 2027).
  4. If the result is 5, it has no trigram of its own: men use Kua 2 (Kun) and women use Kua 8 (Gen).
  5. If you were born in January or the first days of February (before Li Chun, around 4 February), the feng shui solar year has not yet begun, so the previous year is used — tick the box above.

How to use your four lucky directions

Sheng Qi (Generating Qi) — wealth & vitality

The strongest of the four. Point the head of your bed this way, or face it at your desk, when career momentum and income are the priority.

Tian Yi (Heavenly Doctor) — health & recovery

The healing direction. Good for sleep while unwell or run down, and for calm rest areas used often.

Yan Nian (Longevity) — relationships & marriage

The relationship direction — the usual first choice for a shared bed and for seating where the household gathers.

Fu Wei (Stability) — clarity & steady growth

Your own trigram's direction; quiet and grounding. Excellent for meditation, study and focused work.

Practical note: the direction means the way the top of your head points while sleeping (or the way you face while seated). Use a compass app from the center of the room. If you share a bed with a different group, the breadwinner or the person with health concerns usually takes priority, and Fu Wei is the gentlest compromise.

Frequently asked questions

Does my Kua number ever change?

No. It is derived from your birth year and gender, so it stays with you for life. What changes year to year is the flying-star overlay of annual energies, which is read separately from your personal Kua.

Why do men and women born in the same year get different numbers?

The classical Lo Shu formula runs in opposite directions for yin and yang birth patterns, which is why the gender term differs. Kua 5 is the most visible case: a man uses Kua 2 (Kun), a woman uses Kua 8 (Gen).

I was born in January — which year do I use?

The feng shui solar year begins at Li Chun, around 3–5 February. If you were born before it, tick the "born before ~4 February" box and the previous year will be used. (Chinese Lunar New Year falls later, around mid-January to mid-February; classical Ba Zhai uses the solar term, Li Chun.)

What if my partner and I are from different groups?

Give priority to the main breadwinner or the person recovering from illness. Otherwise choose a direction that is favorable to both, or use the calmer Fu Wei direction, and support the other person with the placement of their desk or personal seating.

Is the Kua number the same as my Chinese zodiac sign?

No. Your zodiac animal comes from your birth year in the 12-year cycle and describes shared cultural traits; your Kua number uses the Nine-Star/Lo Shu system with gender and gives a personal compass map. You can find your zodiac sign here →
